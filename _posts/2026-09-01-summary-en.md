Chinese MRAM computing startup Hanxu Technology announced its MRAM-based AI inference product roadmap, revealing a first-generation uHBM design with 24 TB/s on-chip bandwidth and an architecture targeting over 2000 Tokens/s decode for 4B-parameter multi-modal models. The company has also completed successful prototype verification of its SpinPU-ED01 chip. The entire codebase and documentation of Wrapture was written by an AI assistant under Graham Dumpleton's direction. Wrapture solves long-standing pain points for Python developers by enabling non-intrusive tracing and mocking of code you do not control, combining two common use cases into a single, configuration-friendly tool. It is still a very young project only a few weeks old as of its release announcement.

## [Apple finds new evidence in OpenAI trade secret suit](https://www.aibase.com/zh/news/30738) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Apple uncovered four key pieces of evidence on former senior engineer Chang Liu's returned MacBook that prove he stole confidential Apple circuit schematics and used them at OpenAI to train an AI agent, strengthening its trade secret lawsuit against OpenAI. The evidence also shows Liu instructed an OpenAI colleague to destroy relevant materials after learning of Apple's internal investigation. This case will set a critical industry precedent for trade secret protection in AI development, and its outcome will shape how technology companies handle employee movement and confidential data used for AI agent training. It addresses the growing risk of sensitive corporate information being permanently absorbed and spread by autonomous AI systems. Liu used the stolen circuit schematic to run simulations in the LTspice engineering tool in March 2025, and told his OpenAI team his AI agent had learned to run LTspice and review simulation results autonomously. The evidence was synced to Liu's MacBook via iCloud from a Mac mini he used, and Apple is now seeking access to that Mac mini for further investigation.

**Background**: LTspice is a free, widely used SPICE-based analog circuit simulation tool developed by Analog Devices that helps engineers test circuit designs before building physical prototypes. ## [Hunyuan 4 relies on open source and talent flow](https://www.aibase.com/zh/news/30742) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Tencent recently launched its new large model Hunyuan 4 (Hy4preview), which has 770B parameters and supports one million token context windows, after reorganizing its relevant division in just four months. The article analyzes that Hunyuan 4 achieved its rapid iteration by building on proven open source technologies and leveraging cross-company talent flow. This case shows that traditional technical barriers for large language models are rapidly eroding due to open source collaboration and talent movement, reshaping the competitive landscape of the global large model industry. It lowers the threshold for latecomers to catch up in the large model race, changing the long-held assumption that only early entrants can maintain exclusive technical advantages. Hunyuan 4 aligns its 700B-class architecture with the proven engineering solution from Z.ai's GLM-5, integrates DeepSeek's open-source DSA sparse attention mechanism and Z.ai's verified cross-layer index reuse to cut up to 75% of index computation, and adopts Microsoft researcher's publicly shared identity hyper-connection (iHC) instead of the complex mHC framework. A key former technical lead from Z.ai and DeepSeek who led the development of GLM-5 and DSA has joined Tencent's Hunyuan team to drive this technology transfer.

**Background**: GLM is a series of open-weight large language models developed by Chinese AI company Z.ai, and GLM-5 is its latest flagship large model. Residual connection is a fundamental algorithm structure for training deep neural networks, and hyper-connection is an improved variant of this structure. DSA, short for DeepSeek Sparse Attention, is an open-source sparse attention mechanism designed to improve the efficiency of processing long context inputs for large language models.
