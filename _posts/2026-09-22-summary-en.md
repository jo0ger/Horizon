## [Critique of AI-generated technical documentation](https://blog.colinbreck.com/i-dont-want-to-read-what-you-didnt-write/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

An essay published on Colinbreck's blog argues that AI-generated documentation that fills in gaps left by the original author cannot effectively convey semantic information and places unnecessary burden on readers. The essay sparked a substantive discussion on Hacker News with 139 comments from community contributors sharing real-world experiences. As AI becomes increasingly widely adopted for generating technical documentation and code development materials, this essay highlights a critical pitfall that many developers and technical writers overlook: AI cannot convey original semantic information the author did not include, which can harm understanding and increase burden during code reviews and engineering work. The core argument of the essay is that writing is fundamentally a transfer of semantic information from the author's brain to the reader's, and LLMs cannot fill in missing semantic information that the original author never wrote. Multiple commenters shared real-world examples of overly verbose AI-generated documentation adding unnecessary work to code review processes.

hackernews · mooreds · Sep 21, 22:30

**Background**: Large language models (LLMs) have recently become a popular tool for assisting technical writing, including automatically generating documentation, summarizing design decisions, and drafting pull request descriptions. Many development teams have adopted AI tools to speed up documentation writing, but few have examined the potential downsides of relying on AI to fill in content the original author did not write.

**Discussion**: Most commenters agreed with the essay's core argument, sharing personal anecdotes of AI-generated documentation adding unnecessary burden to code review and technical communication. One commenter noted that recent updates to popular LLMs have actually led to a drop in output quality rather than improvements, while another pointed out that the essay itself inadvertently exemplified the issue it criticized in its opening sentence.
