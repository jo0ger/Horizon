## [OpenAI claims Navier-Stokes prize solution amid dispute](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/8/on-navier-stokes/) ⭐️ 10.0/10

OpenAI used an unreleased internal model to produce a claimed resolution of the Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness problem, one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems. The claim is accompanied by academic controversy, as two researchers from NYU and Anthropic accused OpenAI of gaining access to their unpublished work after they made a breakthrough on the problem. This is the first claimed solution to a major Millennium Prize Problem generated by AI, demonstrating the transformative potential of large language models in advanced mathematical research. It also sparks discussion about research incentives and intellectual property norms when AI companies compete on open mathematical problems. OpenAI's AI agents took about 88 hours to arrive at the resolution, with an additional 17 hours for Lean formalization and verification via GPT‑6 Astra, consuming approximately 130 billion output tokens for the problem alone. The problem asks to prove whether smooth, globally defined solutions always exist for the three-dimensional Navier–Stokes equations, which describe fluid motion, or whether a counter-example exists. Prior to this 2026 claim, only the Poincaré conjecture had been officially solved among the seven problems.
